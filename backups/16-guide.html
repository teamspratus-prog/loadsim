How many pallets fit?

Floor positions for six pallet sizes across nine transport units, the arithmetic behind every number, and the reasons the figure you actually load is often lower.

The arithmetic

Two divisions and a multiplication

Floor positions come from the interior width and length against the pallet footprint. Nothing else is involved — not the boxes, not the weight, not the ceiling:

positions = floor(interior width ÷ pallet width) × floor(interior length ÷ pallet length)

Both divisions round down, which is where most of the loss lives. A 53' trailer is 99 in wide inside; two 45 in pallets take 90 in and the remaining 9 in fits nothing. The same trailer is 630 in long, which is 13.1 pallets at 48 in — so 13 rows, and 0.1 of a row is wasted.

Height is a separate calculation, and it runs on the loaded pallet rather than the bare one:

loaded pallet height = pallet height + (box layers × box height) tiers = floor(interior height ÷ loaded pallet height)

Total pallets is then positions × tiers. The simulator caps layers and tiers at whatever the ceiling allows and tells you when it has had to.

Worked example

GMA pallets in a 53' trailer

The most asked version of the question, run end to end. Interior 630 × 99 × 110 in; pallet 48 × 40 × 5 in; box 45 × 24 × 18 in.

  1. 99 in of width ÷ 40 in pallet width = 2.47, so 2 pallets across. The 19 in left over is not enough for a third.
  2. 630 in of length ÷ 48 in pallet length = 13.1, so 13 rows deep.
  3. 2 × 13 = 26 floor positions.
  4. Turn every pallet so the 40 in side runs along the trailer: 99 ÷ 48 = 2.06, still 2 across, but 630 ÷ 40 = 15.75, so 15 deep. That is 30 positions — four more, from nothing but orientation.
  5. Four box layers on a pallet stands 5 + (4 × 18) = 77 in. The ceiling is 110 in, so 110 ÷ 77 = 1.4: one tier. Total 26, or 30 turned.
  6. Two tiers need a loaded pallet no taller than 55 in. Two box layers gives 5 + 36 = 41 in, so two tiers stand 82 in and clear the ceiling: 52 pallets, or 60 turned — with the same eight boxes per position.

Step 6 is the one worth sitting with. Splitting the same boxes across two shorter pallets doubles the pallet count without loading a single extra box, and costs 5 in of height to the second deck.

Reality check

Why you will load fewer

Every number above is geometry with nothing in the way. Five things routinely take some back:

  • Weight arrives before volume. Twenty-six pallets at 800 kg each is 20,800 kg, already past the 20,200 kg the simulator checks against. Dense cargo never reaches the position count.
  • Mixed orientations are not modelled. The turned columns above rotate every pallet uniformly. Pinwheel and part-turned patterns can beat both columns, and single-part mode does not attempt them.
  • Securement takes length. Load bars, airbags, dunnage and a gap at the doors all come out of the same 630 in.
  • The door is narrower than the box. Interior width is not aperture width, and a pallet that fits the trailer still has to get through the opening.
  • Usable volume is already discounted. A 40' HC is 2,694 ft³ in theory and the simulator uses 2,529 ft³ — the fill percentage is measured against the smaller figure.
